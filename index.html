India's interstate ration portability network is heavily concentrated along a few routes. Bihar and Uttar Pradesh account for nearly nine in 10 origin transactions, while Delhi remains the country's biggest destination.

In 2025, India recorded 8.87 million interstate transactions through the One Nation One Ration Card system. But these transactions were unevenly distributed across the nation, with them being concentrated in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Delhi. Bihar and Uttar Pradesh together account for 88.45% of 2025 interstate origin transactions, whereas Delhi had a share of 64.22% of all destination transactions.

Two state routes, Bihar to Delhi and Uttar Pradesh to Delhi, together, accounted for 5.16 million transactions, about 58.25% of the national total.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

What is One Nation One Ration Card?

One Nation One Card scheme was implemented in India on 1st January, 2020. Under this Government scheme, beneficiaries of the Public Distribution System can avail their ration under the National Food Security Act (NFSA) from any ration shop of their convenience in any State or Union Territory.

Migrating beneficiaries like migrant labourers, sugarcane workers, tribals, etc. are provided the facility to collect their entitled food grains through e-POS devices in any shop at their place of migration.

Food grains are distributed to beneficiaries using the 12-digit serial number on existing ration cards, authenticated through Aadhaar.

A transaction is not the same as a person.

The dataset records ration transactions, not unique migrants or beneficiaries.

When these transactions are analysed with respect to individual interstate corridors, the concentration becomes more evident. The Bihar-Delhi route alone accounted for around 2.8 million transactions, followed by the Uttar Pradesh-Delhi with 2.36 million in the year 2025.

The other top routes were Uttar Pradesh-Maharashtra, Bihar-Haryana, Uttar Pradesh-Haryana, and Bihar-Maharashtra.

OVER TIME

The network has grown — but its concentration has remained.

The amount of interstate ration-portability transactions has increased rapidly since 2020. Recorded transactions jumped from 18,140 in 2020 to 8.87 million in 2025.

But the dominance of the origins of transactions has not changed over these years. Bihar and Uttar Pradesh together accounted for about 88.95% of transactions in 2021, 88.55% in 2022, 88.43% in 2023, 87% in 2024 and 88.45% in 2025.

The dominance of these two states has persisted, along with the growth in the volume of portability transactions.

THE DESTINATION

Delhi sits at the centre of the network.

About 90% of Delhi's 2025 interstate transactions originated in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. So Delhi's dominance among destinations is closely tied to both these states. Of all the interstate portability transactions amounting to 5.69 million recorded in Delhi in 2025, 2.80 million were from Bihar and another 2.36 million from Uttar Pradesh.

Thus, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh dominate the origins, and Delhi dominates the destinations, making the connections between these states and Delhi the two largest routes.

Do ration-portability transactions correspond with the country's interstate mobility patterns?

The dominance of these ration-portability transactions along these routes poses a question: Do the ration-portability transactions correspond with the country's interstate mobility patterns?

The One Nation One Ration Card system was introduced to allow beneficiaries who migrate to other states to keep accessing their allowed rations. But this data cannot establish why a beneficiary is in another state or whether the transaction represents a worker or another form of interstate movement.

Thus, the data should be analysed alongside migration and labour-market data, as well as the experiences of individuals accessing the system.

The data shows where.

It does not explain why.

The data shows the volume of interstate transactions and when it rises and falls, but fails to explain why. Other data, such as seasonal migration, employment cycles, administrative factors and other factors, should be analysed alongside to explain the reasons behind.
